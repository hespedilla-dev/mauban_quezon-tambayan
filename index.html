    1. Cagbalete Island

    Nestled off the coast of Mauban in Quezon Province, Cagbalete Island is a tranquil tropical sanctuary famous for its dramatic tides, expansive white sandbars, and lush coastal ecosystems.

    What Makes Cagbalete Special

    The Low Tide Transformation

    Cagbalete is best known for its extreme low tide phenomenon. As the water recedes up to a kilometer from the shore during low tide, it reveals vast sandbars carved with ripple patterns, shallow tide pools, and hidden ecosystems. Walking out into the shallow sea during low tide feels like stepping onto a sprawling, submerged desert of fine cream-colored sand.

    Coastal Flora & Mangroves

    Unlike typical palm-fringed islands, Cagbalete’s shoreline is lined with towering agoho trees (Australian pine) alongside lush mangrove forests. The mangroves act as a natural habitat for diverse marine life, sea birds, and small crabs, offering scenic spots for kayaking or boat tours at high tide.

    Laid-Back, Eco-Friendly Atmosphere

    Cagbalete retains a rustic, laid-back vibe with eco-resorts, bamboo cottages, and beachfront campsites. It serves as a peaceful escape for travelers seeking a slower pace—whether watching sunrise over Lamon Bay, stargazing under clear night skies, or wading through lukewarm shallow waters.


    2. Rizal Hill Park

    Perched on a 27-meter rocky outcrop right at the heart of the poblacion, Rizal Hill Park (historically known as Calvario) is Mauban's most iconic historical landmark and scenic viewpoint.


    Originally part of the foothills of the Sierra Madre range, the hill was isolated when the surrounding land was cut away to build roads. Because of its strategic vantage point overlooking the entire town and Lamon Bay, it served critical roles across centuries of Philippine history:

    • Spanish Era: Known as Calvario, it was utilized as a look-out post against sea raids and a venue for religious activities.
    • American & Japanese Eras: Served as a military command post for American troops, the Philippine Constabulary, and later the Civilian Emergency Administration during WWII.
    • Monument: In December 1925, a prominent monument dedicated to Dr. José Rizal was erected at the summit, transforming the site into a park. It is officially recognized with a Level II Historical Marker by the National Historical Commission of the Philippines (NHCP).


    What to See & Do

    1. The Climb & Garden Path

    Visitors climb a set of stone concrete stairs winding up the rocky hill. The path is lined with lush greenery, stone railings, and shaded benches, giving it a peaceful, elevated garden atmosphere.

    2. The Jose Rizal Monument

    At the highest point stands the historic statue of Dr. José Rizal looking out over the town. It’s a quiet space popular for reflection, light photo stops, and civic gatherings

    3. Panoramic Views

    The top offers a 360-degree view of Mauban—from the heritage roofs and grid of the poblacion to the blue waters of Lamon Bay and the distant silhouette of Cagbalete Island on clear days. It is particularly popular during early mornings and late afternoons for catching the sea breeze and sunset.

    3. Gat-Uban Parl

    Situated along the scenic coastal edge of Mauban, Quezon, Gat-Uban Park is a historic sea-front park dedicated to the town’s legendary founder, Gat Uban—the heroic leader from whom Mauban derives its name.

    Highlights & Atmosphere

    • Iconic Monument: At the center of the park stands a prominent statue of Gat Uban holding a spear, honoring the local bravery and heritage of the municipality.
    • Lamon Bay Views: Situated directly by the water, the park offers panoramic views of the sea, making it a peaceful spot to catch the coastal breeze or watch the sunset.
    • Community Promenade: Featuring paved walkways, landscaped greenery, and seating, it serves as a central hub for locals and visitors alike to relax, stroll, and enjoy street food in the late afternoon.
