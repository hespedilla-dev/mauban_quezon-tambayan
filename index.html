    1. Cagbalete Island

    Nestled off the coast of Mauban in Quezon Province, Cagbalete Island is a tranquil tropical sanctuary famous for its dramatic tides, expansive white sandbars, and lush coastal ecosystems.

    What Makes Cagbalete Special

    The Low Tide Transformation

    Cagbalete is best known for its extreme low tide phenomenon. As the water recedes up to a kilometer from the shore during low tide, it reveals vast sandbars carved with ripple patterns, shallow tide pools, and hidden ecosystems. Walking out into the shallow sea during low tide feels like stepping onto a sprawling, submerged desert of fine cream-colored sand.

    Coastal Flora & Mangroves

    Unlike typical palm-fringed islands, Cagbalete’s shoreline is lined with towering agoho trees (Australian pine) alongside lush mangrove forests. The mangroves act as a natural habitat for diverse marine life, sea birds, and small crabs, offering scenic spots for kayaking or boat tours at high tide.

    Laid-Back, Eco-Friendly Atmosphere

    Cagbalete retains a rustic, laid-back vibe with eco-resorts, bamboo cottages, and beachfront campsites. It serves as a peaceful escape for travelers seeking a slower pace—whether watching sunrise over Lamon Bay, stargazing under clear night skies, or wading through lukewarm shallow waters.
